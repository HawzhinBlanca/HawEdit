## Product contract

Given an unfamiliar Sorani source and a small editorial brief, HawEdit should decide what is worth saying, assemble only the speech required to say it faithfully, and deliver a watchable short plus a replayable professional edit plan. The user should review a finished editorial proposal, not compensate for missing direction by programming cuts.

The brief needs only audience, editorial purpose, output type (standalone highlight / story cutdown / highlights anthology), approximate duration range and tone. Default to original speech and original source footage. Music, added sound, invented footage, a timeline editor, publishing tools and preset expansion are outside this program.

A professional edit plan must explain the actual decisions: central point, viewer question, source evidence, exact kept words/spans, required setup, intended ending, omitted context and its risk, each cut's purpose, original shot continuity, caption treatment and unresolved uncertainty. It must drive the render. A rationale written after a handcrafted render is not that plan.

## Preserve the useful foundation

Reuse canonical transcripts and alignment, the full-transcript Path A, visual retrieval, existing judge abstraction, assembly and source-time mapping, captions, delivery/promotion infrastructure and decision history. Reuse existing story/shot/critique types where they fit, but earn their semantics with real producers and consumers.

Keep the current Python/FFmpeg stack. A larger model fleet, framework rewrite or set of nominal agents is not the first step. Use the existing Kurdish reasoning provider for meaning; deterministic code handles timings, identity, constraints and rendering. Any change to the frozen model policy must follow BLUEPRINT §1/§7 and measured §8 evidence, with an ADR. D-262 already documents the danger of mistaking assembly scaffolding for integrated behavior; D-263 establishes output reconciliation; D-266 covers silence tightening; D-271 covers independent visual retrieval.

## Delivery sequence

### P0 — Make success mean something observable

Close the reproduced missing-video critic approval, missing-detector success and unconditional report approvals. Separate scheduled inspection, completed measurement, semantic judgment and human approval. Require actual output/source hashes and decoded evidence for an output verdict. Prove the final report becomes invalid if its MP4 or plan changes.

Connect the dashboard to a real file registration/upload and the real workflow. Progress comes from completed stage events; results appear only for that source and run. Surface named errors and intentional refusals. Distinct candidate IDs must point to distinct plans and matching media. Store jobs durably through the existing workflow infrastructure.

Retain demo samples only in an unmistakable example view. Do not attach their ranks or quality claims to a new job. Remove unsupported public virality scores and replace them with a short, sourced explanation of why the candidate is worth reviewing.

**Exit:** missing source cannot start; a missing/changed video cannot pass; two unrelated sources cannot silently return the same old clip; restarting the UI reconnects to real state. This establishes truthfulness, not creative excellence.

### P1 — Establish a human editorial benchmark before tuning

Build a development set of 20 episodes and a calibration set of 10, with episode/speaker/source separation where feasible. Reserve 60 unseen test episodes across serious testimony, interviews/Q&A, explanations, debate/opinion and conversational narrative. Include quiet hooks, late-episode peaks, overlap, names, numbers, dialect variation and difficult camera changes. Counts are proposed planning targets; a pilot and power analysis may increase the required independent episodes.

Have native Sorani reviewers identify worthwhile story units, dependencies, risky omissions and invalid joins in the original sources before seeing system alternatives. Use at least two experienced editors to create comparison edits under the same brief, source access and recorded time budget; use three independent native-Sorani reviewers for blinded output assessment and adjudicate disagreements. Editors' outputs and reference transcripts are not labels generated by HawEdit.

Episode 29 is development material only. Set up OpusClip, Vizard and Klap comparison arms with the same source and brief, recording unsupported-language refusals and permitted manual intervention. Run native-input and corrected-transcript arms separately where supported. Do not count unsupported inputs as a semantic editing score, and do not omit them from workflow success accounting.

**Exit:** frozen splits, rubric, comparison rules and source rights; independent references for the development set; untouched holdout. Benchmark preparation is not benchmark success.

### P2 — Understand the entire episode before selecting clips

Produce an evidence-linked account of the episode: people and referents, major claims, events, chronology, questions/answers, corrections, contrasts, consequences and emotional turning points. Attach each proposition to canonical words and source time, with uncertainty. Support speech-only relations: stillness is not lack of content, and narrative meaning must not require an invented visual event.

Maintain full-source coverage. Propose candidates from beginning, middle and end before limiting the number of outputs. Visual retrieval may add discoveries; it must not suppress an extraordinary spoken idea. Inspect uncertain speech and salient visual/audio moments more densely instead of treating uniform snapshots as complete observation.

**Exit:** planted late-episode highlights are discoverable; pronouns and quoted speakers resolve or remain explicitly uncertain; unresolved ASR entities cannot become confident headlines. Judge factual consistency against the original context, not solely a summary of it.

### P3 — Choose and construct the right editorial unit

For each candidate, write a one-sentence central point and viewer question. Identify the strongest truthful opening, minimum required setup, causal/argument chain and supported ending. Generate a small bounded set of substantially different paper edits, not dozens of superficial variants. Compare them against the complete context and reject alternatives requiring invented links.

Use rhetorical forms appropriate to the material: question→answer, claim→evidence→qualification, event→consequence, or setup→revelation→meaning. Do not force every conversation into conflict/climax labels. A low-energy line can be the best hook; a conclusion is not necessarily the last sentence.

Make three output contracts explicit:

- Standalone highlight: one complete thought with its necessary setup and landing.
- Story cutdown: noncontiguous speech with verified dependencies, chronology and attribution preserved.
- Highlights anthology: distinct complete moments, honestly signalled as separate; no claim that unrelated events form one causal chain.

For the current episode example, decide between the displacement/fear story and the UN representative story, or make a clearly separated anthology. Do not bridge an unfinished “because” into a different topic. Restore the dependent continuation, select another boundary, or reject that candidate.

**Exit:** blinded readers can identify the point, subject and ending without needing the omitted episode. Every kept span has a function and every risky omitted span has a disposition. Zero qualifying clips is a valid result when the source has no suitable short; output quotas cannot force filler.

### P4 — Remove verbal waste with contextual speech editing

Work at aligned clause/word boundaries. Identify false starts, stutters, repeated propositions, digressions and hesitation tokens in context. Record each deletion as a source interval plus the reason it is safe. Preserve negation, uncertainty, attribution, contrast, indispensable conjunctions, emotionally meaningful repetition and reactions that carry meaning.

Check the combined sequence after deletions. Two individually complete sentences can still create a false conclusion when joined. ASR punctuation and pause segmentation are candidate boundaries, never sufficient proof of semantic completion.

Verify seam audio from the actual source: avoid cutting consonants, unnatural breaths, clicks and abrupt room-tone changes. Maintain original delivery speed by default. Shorten to the brief by selecting a smaller complete idea before mechanically accelerating or crushing every pause.

**Exit:** independent reviewers judge deletions meaning-preserving and speech natural. Measure removable waste remaining, harmful deletions, semantic drift and audible edit defects separately. No “zero filler” claim from token-list counts alone.

### P5 — Direct pictures and captions around the content

Resolve source shot changes before portrait cropping. Track the relevant speaker or intentional listener reaction; avoid assuming the only visible face is the voice. Hold a stable composition throughout a source shot, then reconsider at its boundary. Check the entire output interval, particularly openings, edits and endings. The current table/window failures become permanent regression cases.

Prefer an original wide shot or an explicitly selected source layout when the portrait crop cannot preserve the relevant person. That is a declared editorial decision, not an invisible fallback. If no honest composition is possible, reject or request review. Never insert a reaction from another moment as if it occurred now.

Use a close-up, hold or reframing change only when it clarifies a turn, emphasis or emotion. Repeated scheduled punch-ins every few seconds do not prove pacing. Caption line/chunk size should follow phrase meaning, phone readability, RTL order and the tone of the story. Serious testimony should not default to playful animation simply because it is short-form content.

**Exit:** no unexplained subject loss in accepted footage; no fabricated reactions; no clipped glyphs or wrong word order; native viewers can read comfortably without missing the picture. Captions, source audio and sidecars agree after every cut.

### P6 — Watch the finished output and repair specific defects

Run an independent inspection path over the encoded MP4 and its decoded audio, plus the relevant original context. The critic receives the delivered pixels/audio and knows which intervals were actually inspected. The planner's confident explanation is not evidence that the render worked.

Assess technical output, selected meaning and viewing experience separately. Technical checks cover decoding, source-clock correspondence, crop/subject continuity, caption order/coverage and audible seams. Editorial review checks promised opening versus delivered payoff, referents, misleading omission, redundant speech and ending quality. Native human listening remains necessary for acceptance until automated semantic/perceptual checks are validated on held-out examples.

Allow at most two targeted repair attempts per candidate initially, with a total latency/cost budget. Re-render, remeasure and rejudge changed bytes. Reject or request review when the budget is exhausted. A repair may not silently rewrite speech or approve its own result. Use the existing exact-output human QC path required by BLUEPRINT §2.

**Exit:** planted crop loss, missing continuation, wrong speaker, unreadable captions and clipped audio are detected in real media; a clean case can also pass. Repair improves blinded preference without changing meaning. Approval remains bound to the exact output.

### P7 — Connect, measure and earn the result

One brief and one source must traverse intake → understanding → candidate comparison → semantic edit → shot/caption plan → render → independent critique/repair → human review/export. No custom per-episode scripts, hand-selected indices or prewritten pass files in the acceptance path.

Keep a small review experience: preview, why this story, included source spans, unresolved issues, alternate editorial direction and exact-output approval. The system should perform editing work; the user should not have to operate a new NLE.

Run unchanged canonical verification, real-media integration, required exact-SHA CI and the held-out benchmark. Then repeat on another untouched set and ordinary owner use. Capture rejection reasons and explicit owner corrections in the existing decision history; distinguish stylistic preferences from factual corrections. Tune only on development data, version changes and retain a rollback baseline.

Make taste adaptation explicit: collect preferred/rejected paper edits and finished cuts with reasons such as weak hook, missing context, artificial pacing, repetition or wrong emotional tone. First test retrieval of approved examples and rubric changes against a frozen baseline; consider a learned ranker only when enough independent labels exist to show a held-out gain. Compare the same system with and without those changes. A log of feedback alone is not learning, and more examples from episode 29 cannot demonstrate transfer to new speakers and topics.

**Exit:** the claimed capability is independently preferred on unseen material and repeatable through the actual app. “Better than professional editors” is permitted only for the tested task, genres and evaluation conditions.

## Proposed acceptance targets—not current results

Predeclare targets before running the test set. Calibrate sample size and reviewer agreement on development material; do not move thresholds after seeing holdout results.

| Outcome | Proposed decision rule |
|---|---|
| Meaning and truth | No confirmed critical fabrication, attribution reversal, dropped negation or false causal join in accepted holdout outputs; report denominator and statistical uncertainty. |
| Standalone story quality | At least 95% of accepted outputs judged understandable and complete without omitted context; separately report publishable coverage of eligible sources to prevent rejection gaming. |
| Professional usefulness | At least 90% of eligible source tasks yield an output editors would publish without structural re-editing; routine title preference is separate from a failed edit. |
| Natural speech | At least 95% of accepted outputs have no material audible splice defect or harmful removal; report remaining removable waste separately. |
| Visual finish | No confirmed critical wrong crop/subject loss in accepted outputs; record duration and severity of lesser defects, not just average face presence. |
| Competitive result | Blind preference over each comparator and the professional-editor arm with an episode-clustered 95% interval whose lower bound exceeds 50%; disclose ties, abstentions, review effort and costs. This is evidence of preference within scope, not universal superiority. |
| Operational reliability | At least 99 of 100 supported-source runs terminate with an accurate result or explicit eligible refusal within the declared budget; also report successful deliverable yield so refusals cannot inflate reliability. |
| Repeatability | Replicate on a second untouched cohort and four weeks of representative owner use; disclose residual failures and any manual rescue. |

Primary outcome is publishable, meaning-faithful output per eligible source, supported by blinded preference. Retention/completion on real viewers can be a later matched experiment; platform engagement alone is confounded by topic, account, audience and distribution and is not a correctness certificate.

A bounded 10/10 judgment becomes reasonable only after these results agree, critical defects are closed, and independent replication supports the same conclusion. It is not guaranteed by implementing this plan.
